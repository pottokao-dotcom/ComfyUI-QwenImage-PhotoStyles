### Style: Color Behind Glass
Look: a painterly, muted colour photograph with soft, fine vintage slide-film grain — low contrast, with small saturated accents of deep red or amber floating in quiet greys and greens.
Lens language: a long lens that compresses the layers and shoots through or past something — a fogged or rain-streaked window, a reflection, a blurred foreground shape; the subject small, partial and off-centre behind veils of blur, patiently waited for.
Light: soft, diffuse light of rain, snow or dusk, filtered through glass, mist or condensation, with glowing reflections.
Emotion: patient, humble, contemplative, stated softly — the world half-hidden and unhurried, beauty waiting quietly at the edge of the frame.
People: when a person is present, they are seen through or past something, small and partial, a quiet indistinct figure rather than a clear portrait.
Scene & props: ordinary city weather and glass — windows, awnings, umbrellas, steam, reflections; large areas of veiled blur.
Colour: muted, low-contrast colour with one or two small saturated accents; keep any colours the user names but render them quietly.
Exceptions to "Throughout": the positional phrases may place veiled blur, mist or soft blocks of colour rather than distinct objects, and things behind glass may be described as indistinct rather than enumerated.
Opening words: the opening sentence calls the image a soft, muted colour photograph seen through glass.
Keep, however detailed the brief: a layer of glass, reflection or blurred foreground between camera and subject, soft low contrast, and muted colour with one or two small saturated accents.
Default ratio when the user gives none: 2:3.
Negative: clean, smooth, denoised, low noise, sharp digital look, high contrast
Grain lead: A soft, muted colour photograph carrying the clearly visible fine grain of vintage slide film.
Grain closer: Throughout, a soft, clearly visible layer of fine colour-film grain covers the image, keeping the muted colours slightly hazy and analog.

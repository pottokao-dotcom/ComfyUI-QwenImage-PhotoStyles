### Style: Geometry of Light
Look: a dramatic black-and-white photograph with fine, smooth medium-format grain and clean silver-gelatin tones; extreme chiaroscuro.
Lens language: a normal-to-slightly-long lens and a patient, composed eye, often from a raised or distant vantage that turns the space into flat geometric planes and makes people tiny within it; the frame is built, never grabbed.
Light: a single hard shaft of directional light cutting through haze or dust, throwing long diagonal shadows; the rest falls into deep black.
Emotion: poetic solitude and hushed, almost spiritual stillness, stated gently — a quiet order found in the everyday.
People: when a person is present, they are small and off-centre, a lone silhouette crossing a vast bright shaft, dwarfed by the geometry around them.
Scene & props: bare architecture, stairs, walls, alleys and the beam itself; large calm areas of shadow left empty.
Colour: unless the user names a colour, the image is monochrome — describe tones as inky black, silvery grey, chalky white, never colour names. If the user names colours, keep them and render them with the same contrast and grain.
Exceptions to "Throughout": large areas of shadow or haze count as content — the positional phrases may place darkness, haze or a beam of light rather than objects.
Opening words: the opening sentence calls the image a high-contrast black-and-white photograph built on a single shaft of light.
Keep, however detailed the brief: one hard, directional beam of light with long cast shadows, deep blacks, and fine grain.
Default ratio when the user gives none: 1:1.
